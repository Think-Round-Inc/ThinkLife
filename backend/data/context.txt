==================================================
ABOUT THINK ROUND
==================================================

Think Round, Inc. is a nonprofit organization founded in July 2004 by artist and educator Heidi Hardin.
The organization developed from community-based arts and educational work in Southeast San Francisco.
Think Round brings together art, science, education, environmental awareness, families, and communities.
Its work is designed to encourage creativity, learning, participation, and stronger community connections.
Think Round works with children, youth, families, artists, educators, schools, and community organizations.
The organization has developed educational programs, community art activities, exhibitions, and environmental initiatives.
Its programs provide opportunities for participants to explore ideas through creative and educational experiences.
Think Round's work recognizes the importance of families and neighborhoods in children's learning and development.
Art and science are used as tools for helping participants explore themselves, their communities, and their environment.
The organization also encourages understanding among people from different cultural and social backgrounds.
Think Round's broader work includes initiatives connected with the Center for the Human Family and the Paradise Project.
A central idea expressed throughout Think Round's work is: "Earth is home. Humans are family."


==================================================
THINK ROUND MISSION AND VALUES
==================================================

Think Round's mission is to demonstrate life's fullest potential through the arts and sciences.
Its mission particularly considers places where environmental concerns intersect with families, homes, and neighborhoods.
The organization values creativity as an important part of individual learning and community development.
Think Round connects artistic expression with science, environmental awareness, education, and community participation.
Its work emphasizes the relationship between individuals, families, communities, cultures, and the environment.
Think Round encourages people to explore both their differences and their shared experiences through creative activities.
The organization promotes opportunities for children and families to participate actively in their communities.
Its programs encourage curiosity, imagination, communication, learning, and creative expression.
Think Round also explores cultural identity, spirituality, environmental responsibility, and human relationships.
Collaboration with artists, educators, families, schools, nonprofits, and community partners is an important part of its work.
The concept of a universal human family is reflected across many of Think Round's projects and educational activities.
The philosophy "Earth is home. Humans are family" summarizes the organization's emphasis on human and environmental connection.


==================================================
THINK ROUND PROGRAMS
==================================================

Think Round develops programs that combine art, science, environmental education, learning, and community participation.
The Children's Mural Program is one of the organization's important community-based educational initiatives.
The program uses visual art to help children explore and understand environmental issues affecting their communities.
Environmental science concepts are combined with basic art instruction and collaborative creative activities.
Children are encouraged to communicate their thoughts, observations, and experiences through visual expression.
The Children's Mural Program developed partly in connection with environmental concerns surrounding Hunters Point Shipyard.
The program makes complex environmental topics more accessible to children and young people through creative learning.
Think Round also developed KEEP!, the Kid's Environmental Education Program.
KEEP! provides hands-on educational experiences related to environmental topics and community awareness.
Participants can explore subjects involving air, water, soil, environmental justice, and their surrounding environment.
Think Round's programs demonstrate how art can complement science and other forms of education.
Through these programs, children and families can participate in creative activities while developing environmental and community awareness.

TRAUMA AND EMOTIONAL DEVELOPMENT
==================================================

Trauma and chronic stress can influence children's learning, behavior, and emotional development.
Fear, instability, or prolonged stress can cause a child's nervous system to focus more strongly on protection and survival.
Under these conditions, concentrating on learning and processing new information can become more difficult.
Stress and trauma can affect attention, memory, organization, impulse control, emotional regulation, and problem-solving.
Learning difficulties related to trauma do not necessarily mean that a child lacks intelligence or ability.
A sense of safety can play an important role in supporting learning and healthy emotional development.
Stable relationships with caring and emotionally responsive adults can provide children with valuable support.
Predictable routines and supportive environments can help children develop greater feelings of safety and stability.
Think Round emphasizes creativity, belonging, relationships, reflection, and expression within supportive environments.
Creative activities can provide children with additional ways to communicate experiences that may be difficult to express verbally.
Art, storytelling, music, movement, and imaginative activities can create opportunities for expression, reflection, and connection.
Supportive relationships and creative opportunities can help children rebuild curiosity, resilience, emotional awareness, and engagement with learning.

STRESS AND ITS EFFECTS ON EVERYDAY LIFE
==================================================

Stress is a normal physical and emotional response to challenging or demanding situations.
Short-term stress can occur when people face changes, uncertainty, responsibilities, or difficult experiences.
When stress continues for a long period, it can affect both mental and physical well-being.
Stress may influence mood, sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, and decision-making.
Some people may feel worried, frustrated, overwhelmed, restless, or emotionally exhausted when under stress.
Stress can also produce physical reactions such as headaches, muscle tension, or changes in sleep patterns.
Different people may experience and respond to stress in different ways.
Recognizing personal stress triggers can help individuals understand when additional coping strategies may be useful.
Maintaining emotional well-being can support a person's ability to handle everyday challenges and responsibilities.
Supportive relationships can help people feel connected and less isolated during stressful periods.
Healthy coping strategies can reduce the impact of stress and support resilience over time.
Persistent or severe stress that interferes with everyday functioning may be a reason to seek additional support.


==================================================
HEALTHY COPING AND SELF-CARE
==================================================

Healthy coping involves using constructive strategies to manage stress and support emotional well-being.
Taking slow, deep breaths or practicing relaxation techniques can help calm the mind and body during stressful moments.
Writing or drawing about feelings can provide a way to express emotions and understand stressful experiences.
Taking breaks from news, social media, or other sources of distress can be helpful when they increase anxiety or stress.
Maintaining a regular daily routine can create structure and predictability during difficult periods.
Physical activity such as walking, stretching, or other enjoyable movement can support overall well-being.
Getting adequate sleep and maintaining healthy eating habits can help support physical and emotional health.
Spending time outdoors or participating in enjoyable hobbies can provide opportunities for relaxation and recovery.
Connecting with trusted friends, family members, or supportive communities can reduce feelings of isolation.
People may benefit from identifying which coping techniques work best for their individual needs and circumstances.
Small and consistent self-care practices can contribute to greater resilience and emotional balance over time.
Coping strategies are supportive tools, but they do not replace professional care when significant mental health concerns are present.


==================================================
WHEN ADDITIONAL SUPPORT MAY BE NEEDED
==================================================

Stress and difficult emotions are common experiences, but sometimes additional support may be helpful.
People should pay attention when emotional or behavioral changes persist or begin interfering with everyday responsibilities.
Difficulty sleeping for an extended period may be one indication that additional support should be considered.
Significant changes in appetite, energy, concentration, or motivation can also be signs that a person may need help.
Losing interest in activities that were previously enjoyable may be another reason to speak with a professional.
Persistent irritability, frustration, restlessness, sadness, or feelings of hopelessness should not be ignored.
Difficulty completing normal work, school, family, or daily activities can indicate that distress is affecting functioning.
NIMH recommends considering professional help when severe or distressing symptoms continue for approximately two weeks or longer.
A primary care provider can be a starting point for discussing mental health concerns and identifying appropriate next steps.
Talking with trusted family members, friends, or other supportive people can also make it easier to ask for help.
Seeking professional support is an appropriate step when self-care strategies are not enough to manage ongoing distress.
Mental health information provided by Zoe should support awareness and education rather than replace evaluation or treatment from qualified professionals.
